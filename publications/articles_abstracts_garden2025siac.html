Abstract

Vortex-induced vibration is a widely studied phenomenon in the offshore industry, particularly in the dynamics of structures such as risers. It arises from the nonlinear interaction of the flow around a cylindrical structure, which produces a von Kármán vortex street. The vortex wake excites the structure, causing it to vibrate transversely to the fluid velocity. These vibrations can lead to structural failure through fatigue. This study examines the physical problem in a two-dimensional spatial domain, treating the structure as a rigid body. To accurately analyze the dynamics of the phenomenon, the dimensionless Navier-Stokes equations are formulated in an arbitrary Lagrangian-Eulerian (ALE) reference frame. This approach accommodates mesh motion and effectively describes the complex fluid-structure interaction. The finite element method (FEM) is used for spatial discretization with a five-node quadrilateral element that automatically satisfies the Ladyzhenskaya-Babuška-Brezzi (LBB), or inf-sup, condition. A first-order semi-Lagrangian (SL) scheme is used to stabilize the numerical code and permit simulations at higher Reynolds numbers and larger time steps, since the method is unconditionally stable, as described by Anjos (2020, 2022). The search-and-interpolation algorithm used in the SL scheme is efficient because it employs suitable data structures to manage the computational mesh. Several tests were performed to investigate the accuracy of the proposed methodology. Flow around a cylinder and other geometries was investigated, and the evolution of the flow pattern caused by vortex shedding is presented. This study provides a better understanding of fluid-structure interaction dynamics and supports the development of strategies to mitigate this phenomenon.
